# MediGraph: An Interactive Network Pharmacology Platform using LLM-RAG and Explainable AI for Drug Repurposing

## Abstract
The traditional drug discovery pipeline is notoriously time-consuming and cost-prohibitive, prompting an industry-wide shift toward computational drug repurposing. While machine learning models demonstrate high accuracy in predicting novel Drug-Target Interactions (DTIs) and Drug-Drug Interactions (DDIs), their clinical adoption remains severely hindered by the "black-box" problem—a lack of transparency in how algorithmic predictions translate to biological reality. To address this interpretability gap, we introduce **MediGraph**, a full-stack, real-time bioinformatics platform that pairs classical predictive modeling with multi-layered Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI). MediGraph leverages RDKit for molecular feature extraction and utilizes ensemble frameworks to predict interaction probabilities. Crucially, the system abandons static prediction outputs in favor of a dual-layered explainability engine: statistical feature attribution is visualized using SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations), while biological validity is established via a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) backend. The RAG pipeline actively queries the PubMed database to ground Large Language Model (LLM) synthesized rationales in the latest peer-reviewed literature. Facilitated by a real-time, interactive React-based graphical interface and a Firebase cloud synchronization layer, MediGraph allows researchers to visualize predictive network pharmacology dynamically. By bridging raw computational inference with transparent, literature-backed medical reasoning, MediGraph serves as a robust and highly interpretable ecosystem for accelerating drug repurposing discovery.

## 1. Introduction

The identification of novel therapeutic indications for approved or investigational drugs, known as drug repurposing, represents a critical strategy in modern pharmacology. By capitalizing on existing safety profiles and pharmacokinetic data, repurposing dramatically curtails the multi-billion-dollar costs and lengthy timelines associated with *de novo* drug design. Consequently, computational approaches—specifically the prediction of Drug-Target Interactions (DTIs) and Drug-Drug Interactions (DDIs) using Machine Learning (ML)—have become indispensable outposts in network pharmacology. These models utilize massive chemical databases, such as ChEMBL and DrugBank, to extrapolate hidden interactions based on molecular fingerprints. However, despite achieving state-of-the-art predictive accuracy, the deployment of these AI models in clinical decision-making is heavily constrained by their "black-box" nature. Medical professionals and researchers are hesitant to act on uninterpretable statistical likelihoods without understanding the underlying biological mechanisms driving those predictions.

As regulatory bodies demand greater transparency in AI-assisted pharmacology, the field has increasingly pivoted toward Explainable AI (XAI). Current post-hoc explanation frameworks, such as SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations), have proven adept at quantifying the contribution of specific molecular features to an algorithmic outcome. Simultaneously, the rapid evolution of Large Language Models (LLMs) offers unprecedented capabilities in biomedical reasoning. Yet, native LLMs are prone to "hallucinations"—generating confident but scientifically inaccurate associations—which is unacceptable in pharmaceutical research. The advent of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) mitigates this by anchoring the LLM's generative process strictly to retrieved factual contexts, such as live academic literature. 

In this paper, we present **MediGraph**, an end-to-end interactive framework that resolves the interpretability bottleneck in computational drug repurposing by unifying predictive ML, dynamic knowledge graphs, localized SHAP analysis, and RAG-augmented synthesis. Built upon a scalable cloud architecture using React and Firebase, MediGraph fundamentally transforms the user experience from passive data observation to active network exploration. When the ML pipeline predicts a novel interaction, the system calculates feature-level SHAP values to expose the statistical reasoning. Simultaneously, the platform's FastAPI backend triggers a RAG pipeline that scrapes the Entrez (PubMed) database for recent academic abstracts pertaining to the specific sub-graph entities. An integrated LLM synthesizes this retrieved literature, providing researchers with a professional, human-readable mechanism of action. 

By delivering statistical transparency alongside literature-grounded biological validation within a real-time visual graph, MediGraph pioneers a comprehensive, "glass-box" approach to network pharmacology. This architecture not only identifies unseen repurposing opportunities but, more crucially, generates the interpretable evidence necessary to justify subsequent wet-lab validation.
